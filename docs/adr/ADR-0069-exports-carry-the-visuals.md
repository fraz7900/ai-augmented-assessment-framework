# ADR-0069: The exports carry the visuals, and the interpretation is written once

**Status:** Accepted
**Sprint:** 23
**Deciders:** Fraz Ahmed
**Related:** ADR-0013 (the deliberate PDF-narrative / XLSX-appendix split this respects), ADR-0066
(the domain completion chart, and the disclosure this closes), ADR-0068 (the review-progress bar, and
the same disclosure again), ADR-0012 (interpretation computed once, in report_service), R-15

## Context

ADR-0066 shipped the domain completion chart and disclosed, in its own consequences, that the
exports did not get it. ADR-0068 shipped the review-progress bar and disclosed the same thing a
second time — *"true twice over"*.

The MIL-gate sentence was the sharper half of that gap. It is genuinely new information: which
maturity level a domain is blocked on and by how many practices was previously derivable only by
opening a domain and comparing practice MILs by hand. It existed on screen and nowhere else, so a
reviewer who read it and then sent the PDF had sent a document that did not contain it. A disclosure
repeated twice without being fixed stops being a disclosure and starts being a defect.

## Decision

**The PDF gets the visuals. The XLSX gets the numbers behind them.**

That split is not a preference; ADR-0013 already decided it. The PDF is this project's narrative
artifact and the XLSX is *"the working-data appendix: flat, sortable/filterable sheets"*, and that
ADR explicitly warns against pulling the two formats back toward one layout. A native chart in the
spreadsheet would be exactly that, for a reader who already has a charting tool in front of them.

### PDF

- A **Domain Completion** section, with one drawn bar per populated domain, `met/total`, and the
  domain score in its own units. This section is distinct from Complication below it, which lists
  only domains with at least one gap — so a reader of the PDF alone previously could not tell a
  finished domain from one nobody had started.
- A **review-progress bar** in the Situation section, stacked over the counts already printed there.
- The **MIL-gate sentence** under each affected bar.
- The caveat that bars are not the maturity score under `cumulative_mil`, and the opposite statement
  under `coverage`, where the bar and the score genuinely are the same measure.

Drawn with two filled rectangles per bar. No charting dependency: a fill over a track is the whole
requirement, and fpdf2 already draws rectangles.

### XLSX

- A new **Domain Completion** sheet: code, name, practices met, applicable practices, completion as
  a real fraction (so it sorts and charts as a number in the reader's own spreadsheet, not as a
  pre-formatted string), score, blocking MIL, practices blocking, and the gate sentence.
- The **Domain Scores** sheet's header now names the unit. It was a bare `Score`, and that number is
  an ordinal MIL 0-3 under one scoring model and a 0.0-1.0 fraction under the other. A reader
  sorting that column had no way to know which — the same R-15 ambiguity ADR-0066 refused to draw as
  a bar length, sitting unlabelled in a spreadsheet the whole time. Found while writing this
  tranche, not reported.

### The interpretation is composed once, server-side

`DomainProgress` gains `gate_note`, built in `report_service` and rendered verbatim by all three
consumers. ADR-0012 established this for `Situation.so_what` and `DomainGapGroup.so_what` in exactly
these terms: an interpretation written separately in the frontend, the PDF and the XLSX is three
chances to say something different about the same number.

The frontend previously composed that sentence in JSX. It now renders the server's, the way
`ScoreHeadline` already renders its headline, and a test asserts it prints what it is given rather
than rebuilding the wording locally.

## Consequences

- The two dashboard visuals now leave the database with the report, and the gate explanation goes
  with them.
- A domain that is fully met appears in the PDF for the first time. Previously only gapped domains
  were listed, so the best news in an assessment was invisible in the exported document.
- `DomainProgress` gains one optional field. Additive and defaulted; the seal covers the assessment
  record rather than the report (ADR-0060), so tamper-evidence is unaffected.
- 10 new backend tests, all reading the generated bytes back with `pypdf` and `openpyxl` the way a
  real consumer would, per this file's existing convention.
- One existing frontend test changed from asserting the component's own wording to asserting it
  renders the server's. That is the point of the change, so the test moving is correct rather than
  incidental.

## What this does not do

**Rejection is grey in the PDF too.** Precision was measured at 0.012, so declining a proposal is
the expected outcome for most of a queue (ADR-0067), and a report that paints correct review work as
an alarm misreports it on paper exactly as it would on screen. The palette decision from ADR-0068
carries over deliberately rather than being re-argued per medium.

**The XLSX has no chart, and that is the decision rather than an omission.** See ADR-0013 above.

**Bars are still a summary.** Practice counts weight every practice equally. The gap tables below
them are where the detail lives, in both formats.

## Alternatives considered

**A native `openpyxl` BarChart in the spreadsheet.** Rejected on ADR-0013's split, which that ADR
already anticipated being eroded and warned about by name.

**Embedding the frontend's rendered chart as an image.** Rejected: it would make export depend on a
browser or a headless renderer, which is a large new dependency and a new failure mode for a
document that must generate server-side, locally, every time.

**Leaving the gate sentence to each renderer.** Rejected — the whole second half of this ADR. Three
copies of a sentence about a subtle scoring rule is three copies to get wrong, and the project
already made this exact decision once in ADR-0012.

**Printing the raw `domain_scores` dict into the PDF.** Rejected for the reason the XLSX header was
fixed: a number whose unit depends on the framework, printed without it, is an invitation to
misread. Every score in both exports now carries its unit.
